# Experimental MIRA 0.1 — Knowledge Foundation

**Status:** Experimental / Not Approved / General Foundation Candidate

## Purpose

Support problem finding without treating generic knowledge as case fact or as Kuni/MIRA authority.

## Knowledge Element Schema

- Phenomenon
- Generating Mechanism
- Actors / Variables
- Feedback / Delay / Constraint
- Boundary Conditions
- Observable Signals
- Discriminating Evidence
- Common Misdiagnosis
- Intervention Levers
- Counter-example / When not to apply
- Source / Provenance
- Scope / Freshness
- Current-case Applicability Hypothesis

## Foundation Domains

### 1. Problem Finding & Structuring

The presented request, symptom, cause, or solution may not be the problem that must be solved. For messy problems, use multiple representations, boundary choices, and dialogue when they materially change understanding.

Boundary: do not reopen a mature, well-grounded problem without material reason.

### 2. Stakeholder Knowledge, Conflict & Boundary

Different actors may see different problems because they know, value, fear, protect, control, or experience different things. When material, discover:

- what each actor thinks the problem is;
- what they value, fear, or protect;
- what they know that others may not;
- what they include or exclude from the system boundary;
- what local optimum is rational from their position;
- what consequence or exposure they bear;
- who decides, informs, executes, and is affected.

Stakeholder statements are evidence/representations, not automatic truth or authority.

### 3. Dynamic Mechanisms

Repeated or unstable outcomes may be generated by feedback, delay, flow, constraints, or locally rational reactions. Consider:

- what signal an actor observes;
- what action the signal triggers;
- what delay exists before the system responds;
- whether the response alters the next signal;
- whether locally rational actions amplify variation system-wide;
- where stocks, flows, buffers, capacity limits, queues, or bottlenecks exist.

Common misdiagnosis: blaming the last Human actor when the system structure makes the behavior locally rational.

Counter-example: a single local defect with no material feedback or delay may not need system-dynamics analysis.

### 4. Control & Intervention

Once a mechanism is plausible, interventions may target information visibility, feedback design, decision cadence, smoothing/rate of change, buffers, capacity, queu/flow rules, constraints, incentives/KPIs, authority allocation, handoffs, or Human-Machine work allocation.

Mechanism understanding does not automatically determine the best intervention.

### 5. Knowledge Elicitation & External Reuse

Do not reinvent material knowledge that a recipient, stakeholder, organization already has, or that mature external knowledge already explains.

Source routing:

- known in the case → inspect case evidence;
- likely known by a person → ask the minimum material question;
- likely known in the organization → seek relevant record or operational evidence;
- likely a general mechanism → research mature external knowledge;
- not sufficiently known → preserve as hypothesis/unknown and design a discriminating test.

External or past knowledge is a candidate mechanism, not a case fact.

### 6. Corrective & Double-Loop Learning

After execution or feedback, ask both:

1. What did we learn about the problem, mechanism, and intervention?
2. Why did we see or fail to see the problem correctly before?

The second question may update problem-finding capability, not only case knowledge.

## Experimental Application Boundaries

- General foundation ≠ case fact.
- Kuni experience ≠ universal rule.
- Stakeholder representation ≠ decision authority.
- Plausible mechanism ≠ confirmed cause.
- Problem understanding ≀ value realization.
