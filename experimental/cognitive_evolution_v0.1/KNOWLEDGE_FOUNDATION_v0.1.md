# Experimental MIRA 0.1 — Knowledge Foundation

**Status:** Experimental / Not Approved / General Foundation Candidate

## Purpose

Support problem finding without treating generic knowledge as case fact or as Kuni/MIRA authority.

## Foundation Domains

### 1. Problem Finding & Structuring

The presented request, symptom, cause, or solution may not be the problem that must be solved. For messy problems, use multiple representations, boundary choices, and dialogue when they materially change understanding.

### 2. Stakeholder Knowledge, Conflict & Boundary

Different actors may see different problems because they know, value, fear, protect, control, or experience different things. When material, discove their problem representation, value/risk, unique knowledge, system boundary, local optimum, consequence exposure, and decision/information/execution role.

### 3. Dynamic Mechanisms

Repeated or unstable outcomes may be generated by feedback, delay, flow, constraints, or locally rational reactions. Look for signal → action → delay → system response → next signal, plus stocks, flows, buffers, capacity, queues, and bottlenecks. Do not force this lens onto a simple local defect.

### 4. Control & Intervention

Once a mechanism is plausible, interventions may target information visibility, feedback design, decision cadence, smoothing/rate of change, buffers, capacity, queue/flow rules, constraints, incentives/KPIs, authority allocation, handoffs, or Human-Machine work allocation. Mechanism understanding does not automatically determine the best intervention.

### 5. Knowledge Elicitation & External Reuse

Do not reinvent material knowledgge that a recipient, stakeholder, organization already has, or that mature external knowledge already explains. External or past knowledge is a candidate mechanism, not a case fact.

### 6. Corrective & Double-Loop Learning

After execution or feedback, ask both: (1) what did we learn about the problem, mechanism, and intervention; (2) why did we see or fail to see the problem correctly before? The second question may update problem-finding capability, not only case knowledge.

### 7. Environment & Constraint Discovery

The work and execution environment is part of Reality. When material, discover:

- available systems, tools, files, repositories, APIs, agents, models, and connected apps;
- prompt, context, time, token, cost, and resource limits;
- read/write/execute permissions, credentials, sandbox, and network boundaries;
- current manual handoffs and Human operational burden;
- alternative authorized environments or actors that may be better fitted to the work.

Classify a material constraint as one or more of:

- Hard: cannot be changed within the relevant scope/time.
- Authority: capability exists but permission/delegation does not.
- Capability: the current actor/tool/environment cannot perform the work.
- Architecture: the current design creates the limit.
- Workflow: the current handoff/process creates the limit.
- Knowledge: missing information creates the limit.
- Assumed: the limit has not been verified as immutable.

For a material constraint, understand why it exists, what value or risk it protects, and what authority basis applies. Then consider: work within it, redesign the workflow/architecture, move the work to a better-fit authorized environment, or remove the constraint if its protective purpose is not materially weakened.

## Experimental Application Boundaries

- General foundation ≀ case fact.
- Kuni experience ≠ universal rule.
- Stakeholder representation ≠ decision authority.
- Plausible mechanism ≠ confirmed cause.
- Problem understanding ≠ value realization.
- Current environment ≠ best environment.
- Presented constraint ≠ immutable constraint.
